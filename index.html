Nonprofit · Founder

Youth and Families United

My family moved a few miles down Rockville Pike and the school district around me changed completely — different resources, different support, different expectations for what students could do. YAFU started because none of that should turn on an address.

I built the platform from scratch: a web application where families could find resources and request help meeting basic needs, plus a second tool that streamlined volunteer recruitment for local shelters. Writing the code was never the hard part. Getting it in front of people was, which is how I ended up teaching myself SEO and social strategy.

From there it became tutoring — I interviewed tutors to run free virtual sessions — plus food drives, school pantries, and chapters run by students at nearby high schools.

TreeHacks 2024, Stanford · Product lead

NoWaste.ai

Americans throw out around 200lbs of food a person each year, mostly on a guess. NoWaste lets you photograph something, describe what happened to it — "pizza left on a warm counter six hours, then refrigerated for eight" — and get a real read on whether it's still safe, from GPT-4 Vision and Llama 70B.

The decision I'm proudest of was refusing to ship only an app, because an app assumes a smartphone. So we built two things: a lightweight iOS app, and a Raspberry Pi unit with a camera and odor sensors in a housing we made on site, at roughly $100 to assemble, for communities where phones aren't a given.

On a team of four I owned product strategy, split the work by everyone's strengths, and pitched to the judges. It won Best Use of an API.
